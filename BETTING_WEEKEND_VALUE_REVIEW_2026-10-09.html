The deeper checks

Manchester City: modest value, with the price doing the work

Restoring the omitted recent matches changes City’s primary estimate from 51.1% to 49.9%, and its research estimate from 45.8% to 44.5%. The more conservative displayed view implies fair odds of about 2.25, versus the quoted 2.40. An xG-only replay gives 43.6% in the research calculation. Using 18 venue matches in the primary calculation gives 42.1%, barely above the 41.7% threshold. That makes this a plausible small edge rather than a large cushion.

City’s 9 October team update confirms Haaland is fit and Foden suspended. Liverpool’s 7 October update leaves Isak and Gakpo in doubt. Their final availability matters; these statistical forecasts do not explicitly adjust for individual absences. Both clubs also have new managers, limiting how much we should trust last season’s matches. Recheck the available price and confirmed teams before relying on this signal.

Bournemouth: a possible price opportunity, weaker evidence than the headline suggests

The primary view stays at 42.4%; corrected research gives 39.3%. But Bournemouth’s last eight away games contain 7 goals conceded against 14.4 xG conceded. Actual goals allowed are only 0.88 per game versus 1.80 xGA. Our mixed goals/xG calculation rewards that record. We have not established whether it reflects repeatable goalkeeping, opponent finishing, or chance.

When actual finishing is removed, Bournemouth’s win estimate falls to 30.6% in the primary calculation and 31.9% in the research calculation; the longer venue window gives 31.4%. Those diagnostic figures still exceed the 21.1% needed at 4.75, so the signal does not disappear. They are not newly validated forecasts. Six of the eight away matches are from last season, and Bournemouth have only three points from their five current-season games.

Liverpool’s September Bournemouth team preview confirms Marco Rose succeeded Iraola. Chelsea now also have a different coach, as reflected in the club’s current weekly preview. England reported Alex Scott’s withdrawal on 5 October; that does not establish his availability for Chelsea. Full current club squad updates were not verified. Bournemouth therefore remains a speculative value candidate rather than the most dependable selection. The individual match market still showed 15/4 (4.75) when checked.

Arsenal–Leeds draw: worth watching, without overstating the away win

The two displayed views give a draw about 26%, versus the 19.0% needed at 5.25. The diagnostic checks range from 21.8% to 24.3%, also above that threshold. However, Arsenal remain favourites in every calculation. The price difference is not evidence that Leeds are likely to win.

Leeds’ 8 October squad update reports Rodon still rehabilitating, James out, and questions over Bahoya and Wilson. Arsenal’s final squad status was not fully verified. That uncertainty prevents promoting either the draw or the long-priced Leeds win to a high-confidence recommendation.

Forest and Brighton: conditional candidates

Forest’s corrected research estimate is 41.4% against 36.4% needed at 2.75. The primary estimate is much higher, but removing actual finishing from the research calculation reduces Forest to 36.1%, around break-even. The apparent value depends on how much faith we put in recent conversion.

Including Brighton’s missing 5–0 away win over Coventry lifts its displayed views to roughly 47–48%. But an xG-only primary calculation gives 39.3%, and the longer venue window gives 40.2%, both below the 41.7% needed at 2.40. One recent result and the balance between goals and chances have a substantial effect. Neither club’s complete current squad update was verified.

Brentford and Manchester United: no clear enough edge

Brentford’s current Villa preview supports their strong start and describes Villa’s summer rebuild. Our research view favours Brentford, but the primary view favours Villa and puts Brentford below the price’s break-even point. That disagreement matters more than simply selecting the more favourable number.

Restoring United’s omitted 0–1 home derby defeat lowers the primary estimate from 60.1% to 56.2%. At 1.75, United need 57.1%; the research view’s 58.2% leaves only a small margin. It is not a compelling price after the correction.
